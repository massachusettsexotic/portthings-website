Arrival day: the essentials

One terminal, a walkable waterfront and a fixed departure time. Nine things worth knowing before you step off the ship.

  1. 1

    The cruise terminal

    Ships tie up at the cruise terminal on the harbour, at the south end of the Malecón. Downtown is a walk: about 10 minutes along the flat waterfront path to the giant flag and the Plaza Cívica, with Calle Primera and the fish market a few minutes beyond. Taxis and tour vans wait outside the terminal for everything further. Carry your ship card and photo ID to get back in through the port gate.

  2. 2

    Taxis: agree the fare first

    Taxis have no meters and we couldn't confirm an official fare table, so agree the price for the whole car, and the currency, before you get in. For La Bufadora or the wine valley, agree the round trip, the waiting time and when you'll be back at the terminal. Registered tours are the simpler choice for the Valle de Guadalupe.

  3. 3

    What the US State Department says

    The US State Department rates Mexico Level 2, 'Exercise increased caution', and the state of Baja California Level 3, 'Reconsider travel', due to terrorism, crime and kidnapping (advisory issued 11 August 2025). It says criminal groups fight for control in border areas, that travellers should remain on main highways and avoid remote locations, and that there are no additional travel restrictions for US government employees in Baja California state, including Ensenada. Across Mexico it advises not travelling between cities after dark and using taxis from regulated stands or app services rather than hailing on the street. Check travel.state.gov before you go.

  4. 4

    Pesos or dollars?

    Dollars are accepted in many shops, bars and restaurants near the port, but you'll get their exchange rate and often your change in pesos. Pesos are simpler and cheaper at the fish market, taco stands and small shops. Use ATMs inside banks rather than street machines. When a card terminal asks whether to charge in dollars or pesos, choose pesos.

  5. 5

    Check which clock your ship keeps

    Ensenada keeps Pacific time, with daylight saving in summer. Ships sometimes keep a different time on board. The all-aboard time in the daily planner is the one that counts — set a phone alarm by it, not by a clock on shore.
