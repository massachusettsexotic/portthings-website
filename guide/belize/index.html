Arrival day: the essentials

A tender port day in Belize goes best when the first hour is planned. Nine things worth knowing, roughly in order.

  1. 1

    It's a tender port

    Big ships anchor offshore at Belize City and small tender boats carry you to the Fort Street Tourism Village. Tender lines can take a while on busy mornings, and the LAST tender back leaves before all-aboard — your ship's daily sheet gives the time. Norwegian ships that call at Harvest Caye dock at a pier instead. Every distance in this app names the landing it is measured from.

  2. 2

    Licensed taxis — agree the fare first

    Licensed taxis wait at the Tourism Village gate. Before you get in, agree the price — per person or per car, US or Belize dollars, one way or return with waiting time. For anything beyond Fort George, a registered tour or a licensed driver is the easy way.

  3. 3

    Belize time vs. ship time

    Belize keeps Central Standard Time all year (UTC−6), with no daylight saving. Your ship may keep a different clock — all-aboard and the last tender are always on SHIP time. Set a phone alarm on ship time.

  4. 4

    Two dollars to one

    BZ$2 = US$1, fixed by the Central Bank. US dollars are taken almost everywhere, but change may come back in Belize dollars. Always ask which dollars a price is in. Carry small US bills for taxis, tips and stalls.

  5. 5

    Roaming — check before you land

    Belize is not part of most US domestic plans: check your roaming package, or you may pay by the megabyte. Turn off cellular data at sea — the ship's network is expensive.
