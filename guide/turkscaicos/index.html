Arrival day: the essentials

The first day sets up the whole trip. Nine things worth doing, roughly in order.

  1. 1

    Taxi or rental car from the airport

    Providenciales airport is 15 minutes from Grace Bay. Taxis wait outside and charge per person on a fixed schedule — about $30–40 for one or two people to Grace Bay; confirm the total before you get in, and share if you can. There is no Uber. Rental cars (book ahead in winter) are at the terminal; you'll drive on the left. Resort shuttles are the easy answer if yours offers one.

  2. 2

    It's US dollars — and mostly cards

    The Turks and Caicos use the US dollar, so Americans need nothing and everyone else changes once. Cards work almost everywhere on Provo; carry $100–200 in small bills for taxis, the fish fry, beach vendors and the outer islands, where ATMs are rare or absent. ATMs are at the banks (Scotiabank, CIBC FirstCaribbean) and in Graceway IGA. Tip: the ferry, the iguana island fee and North Caicos guides are cash.

  3. 3

    Sort your phone data — roaming here is expensive

    TCI is not in most US or European roaming bundles — check, because $10–15 a day adds up. The easy fix is an eSIM bought online before you land, or a Flow or Digicel prepaid SIM at the airport or in Grace Bay (bring your passport). Coverage is good on Provo and Grand Turk, patchy on North and Middle Caicos. Resort and restaurant Wi-Fi is everywhere on Provo.

  4. 4

    Book the boat day and the good tables

    The one thing that sells out is the half-day boat excursion (Little Water Cay, Half Moon Bay, snorkeling) — book it for your first free morning, directly with an operator. Coco Bistro and the top Grace Bay dinners fill a week or more ahead in winter. The North Caicos ferry, the Thursday fish fry and the reefs need no booking. Kite lessons at Long Bay and Chalk Sound kayaks: a day ahead is plenty.

  5. 5

    Getting around: rent a car, drive on the left

    Provo is spread out and taxis are expensive — $15–25 per person for short hops, metered per person, no Uber. If you'll leave your resort more than twice, a rental car pays for itself ($60–120 a day; book ahead in winter). Driving is on the left, roundabouts everywhere (yield to the right), roads mostly good, unlit at night, and speed limits low — 40 mph on Leeward Highway, 20 in Grace Bay. Outer islands: rental car at the ferry dock on North Caicos, taxis or a jitney on Grand Turk.

Ready-made days

  • Grace Bay Classic

    The beach, two reefs from shore, the Saltmills and a sunset dinner — the perfect first day

  • Chalk Sound & the South Shore

    The lagoon that doesn't look real, Sapodilla Bay, Taylor Bay and conch at Omar's

  • Long Bay, Leeward & the Cays

    Kites at Long Bay, the iguanas of Little Water Cay and the emptiness of Half Moon Bay

  • Blue Hills & the Wild Northwest

    Conch at the source, the oldest settlement on Provo, Malcolm's Road and the Northwest Point wall

  • North & Middle Caicos by Ferry

    Flamingos, a plantation, the cliffs of Mudjin Harbour and the Conch Bar Caves — the islands' best day

  • Grand Turk & Salt Cay

    A 30-minute flight to the capital: Front Street, the museum, the lighthouse, the wall and the stingrays of Gibbs Cay

  • Cockburn Town & the National Museum

    From the pier: the wreck museum, Front Street's verandas and lunch at an old inn

  • Governor's Beach on Foot

    A free, calm beach 20 minutes' walk from the ship, past the space-capsule monument

  • The Lighthouse & the North End

    The 1852 lighthouse on the cliffs, a quiet shore and Front Street, back by 1 PM

  • Stingrays at Gibbs Cay

    A small boat from town to the rays in knee-deep water, back by 12:30

  • Rainy Day on Grand Turk

    The National Museum, island art, lunch at the inn and the port shops — all indoors

  • Rainy Day in Grace Bay

    Bakery breakfast, a bookshop, galleries, a deli lunch and ice cream

  • Grace Bay for Free

    For Grace Bay guests: the public beach, a shore reef with your own mask, Thursday's fish fry

  • Five Cays Conch Crawl

    Lunch off the fishing boats, a swim at Sapodilla Bay, fritters and rum punch at sunset

  • West Caicos Wall by Boat

    A booked two-tank dive from Turtle Cove, then an easy shore snorkel at Smith's Reef

  • The Wild Northwest

    A 4x4 track to an empty beach at the marine park, then conch in Blue Hills

  • Provo's Hidden History

    Plantation ruins, sailors' rock carvings, a calm swim and lunch off the boats

  • A Slow Grace Bay Day for Two

    A sunrise walk, the quiet Leeward end, sunset on a deck and dinner under the palms

  • North Caicos Beaches by Ferry

    Snorkel three rocky cays, an empty north-shore beach and a blue hole
