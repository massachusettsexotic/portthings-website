Where the ships dock

  • Dr. A.C. Wathey Cruise Facility, Philipsburg

Typically 8 AM – 5 PM · all-aboard 30–60 min before sailing

Arrival day: the essentials

A port day here is short and the island is small but slow in traffic. Nine things worth knowing before you step off the ship.

  1. 1

    The pier and the walk to town

    Ships dock at the Dr. A.C. Wathey Cruise Facility at Pointe Blanche. Front Street is 10–15 minutes' walk along the bay, flat but hot; the water taxi from the terminal to the Boardwalk sells all-day tickets (about $6–7). Everything you need for a Philipsburg day is inside that walk.

  2. 2

    Taxis: set fares, agree first

    Taxis wait at the dispatch booth inside the cruise facility. Fares are government-set per destination: one or two people pay a flat fare — $25 to Maho, and on the French side's euro tariff €30 to Marigot and €35 to Grand Case or Orient Bay — while groups of three to five pay a lower rate each. Many are shared vans that leave when full. Confirm the fare with the driver before you get in, agree the pick-up time for the return, and carry small US bills.

  3. 3

    Dollars, euros, guilders

    US dollars work everywhere on the Dutch side and most places on the French side. The Dutch side's official money is now the Caribbean guilder (Cg, 1.79 to the dollar), which replaced the Antillean guilder in 2025 — you won't need any. The French side uses euros; many places take dollars one-for-one, which is a poor rate, so pay by card in euros when you can. ATMs are in Philipsburg, Simpson Bay and Marigot; French-side machines give euros.

  4. 4

    Two countries, no border

    The island has been split between the Netherlands and France since the Treaty of Concordia in 1648, and there is still no checkpoint: a road sign and a change in the road surface are all you'll see. Your passport stays on the ship for the island itself — you need it only for the ferries to Anguilla or St. Barths. The French side keeps French hours, French prices and French power sockets (220 V; the Dutch side is 110 V with US plugs).

  5. 5

    Phones and data

    Check your roaming before you land: the island counts as the Caribbean for most US plans and as France for EU plans only on the French side. Your phone may flip between Dutch-side and French-side networks as you drive. Beach bars on Great Bay and in Orient Bay offer free Wi-Fi with a drink. Everything in this app works without a signal.
