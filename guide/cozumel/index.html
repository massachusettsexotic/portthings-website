Arrival day: the essentials

Three piers, one small island and a fixed departure time. Nine things worth knowing before you step off the ship.

  1. 1

    Which pier are you at?

    Cozumel has three cruise piers. Punta Langosta is right in downtown San Miguel — the town square is about ten minutes' walk. The International Pier (SSA México) and Puerta Maya are next to each other about 4 km south of town, where the big ships usually dock; from there downtown is a 10–15 minute taxi ride. Your ship's daily planner names the pier. Pick it under Back to Ship on the Map so walking minutes and the compass use the right one.

  2. 2

    Taxis: agree the fare before you get in

    Cozumel taxis have no meters and the fares aren't fixed, so agree the price with the driver before you get in: say where you're going and settle the total and the currency (dollars or pesos). As a rough guide, a taxi for one to four people from the southern piers to downtown is about US$10–16. Fares are usually per taxi, not per person, for up to four people; larger groups pay more or take a van. If you're going somewhere remote like the east side or Punta Sur, agree the return fare and a pick-up time too.

  3. 3

    Pesos or dollars?

    US dollars are accepted almost everywhere near the piers, but you'll get the shop's exchange rate and your change in pesos. Pesos are cheaper for small things: tacos, taxis in town, the market. Use ATMs inside banks rather than street machines. When a card terminal asks whether to charge in dollars or pesos, choose pesos — your own bank's rate is almost always better.

  4. 4

    Check which clock your ship keeps

    Quintana Roo stays on UTC−5 all year, with no daylight saving. From March to November that is one hour behind US Eastern time. Ships sometimes keep their own time on board. The all-aboard time printed in the daily planner is the one that counts — set a phone alarm by it, not by a clock on shore.

  5. 5

    The ferry to Playa del Carmen

    Two companies, Winjet and Ultramar, run passenger ferries from the downtown terminal on Avenida Rafael Melgar (at Avenida Juárez, facing the main square) to Playa del Carmen, with departures roughly every 30–90 minutes from about 7 AM into the evening. The crossing takes about 45 minutes and can be rough. A ticket from one company isn't valid on the other's boats, and Ultramar asks you to be at the terminal 30 minutes before departure. Only try it on a long port day: allow for the queue, the crossing both ways and the taxi back to a southern pier. If you miss the last boat that gets you back before all-aboard, the ship will not wait.
