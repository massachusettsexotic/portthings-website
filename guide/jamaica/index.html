Arrival day: the essentials

A port day in Jamaica goes best when the first hour is planned. Nine things worth knowing, roughly in order.

  1. 1

    Know which pier you're at

    Ships dock at Montego Bay (Freeport), at the Historic Falmouth Cruise Port, or in Ocho Rios — at the main cruise pier or at Reynolds Pier just west of it. Every distance in this app is measured from that port's pier and names it. Falmouth and Ocho Rios town are walkable from the gangway; in Montego Bay you'll want a taxi.

  2. 2

    Licensed taxis only — agree the fare first

    Use the licensed taxis and tour drivers at the port's transport desk — JUTA and other registered operators, with red PP licence plates — never a driver who approaches you on the street. Before you get in, agree the price, whether it's per person or per car, in US or Jamaican dollars, and one way or return. Pay at the end.

  3. 3

    Island time vs. ship time

    Jamaica keeps Eastern Standard Time all year, with no daylight saving. From March to November that is an hour BEHIND US Eastern time. Your ship may keep a different clock — all-aboard is always on SHIP time. Set a phone alarm on ship time and leave the beach early.

  4. 4

    US dollars, Jamaican dollars, small bills

    US dollars are accepted almost everywhere visitors go, but change often comes back in Jamaican dollars (about J$158 per US$1 in September 2026). Carry small US bills for taxis, tips and stalls; cards work at attractions, bigger restaurants and shops. Use ATMs inside banks or malls.

  5. 5

    Roaming costs — check before you land

    Jamaica is not part of US domestic plans: check your roaming package, or you may pay by the megabyte. Turn off cellular data at sea — the ship's network is expensive. Calling a Jamaican number: +1 876 or +1 658 and the seven digits.
