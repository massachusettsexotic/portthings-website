Arrival day: the essentials

The first day sets up the whole trip. Nine things worth doing, roughly in order.

  1. 1

    Take the train from Schiphol

    The train station is directly under the airport terminal — follow the signs down. Trains to Amsterdam Centraal leave every few minutes and take 15 minutes; tap any contactless bank card or phone on the gate to get in, and tap out at Centraal. No ticket needed. A taxi costs €50–70 for the same trip. If your hotel is near Museumplein or Leidseplein, bus 397 from the airport drops you closer.

  2. 2

    Cards everywhere — but not all cards

    Amsterdam runs on tap-to-pay, and many places are card-only. The catch: some supermarkets and shops take only debit cards ('PIN only') and refuse credit cards — Albert Heijn is the classic surprise. Carry one debit card plus €30–50 in cash for markets and the odd bakery. Use the yellow Geldmaat ATMs (bank-owned); avoid the blue Euronet machines, and if any ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, decline — always pay in euros.

  3. 3

    Sort your phone data

    EU plans roam free. Everyone else: an eSIM bought online before you land is the easy answer, or a prepaid SIM from a phone shop or Albert Heijn (Lebara, KPN). Coverage is excellent everywhere, including the trains. Free Wi-Fi is common in cafés and on the NS trains.

  4. 4

    Book the big three tonight

    Three places need tickets in advance: the Anne Frank House (online only, released Tuesdays at 10 AM for six weeks ahead — sells out in an hour), the Van Gogh Museum (online only, no ticket desk at all), and the Rijksmuseum (timed entry, book to skip the queue). Doing four or more museums? The Museumkaart (about €75, valid a year, 400+ museums) or the I amsterdam City Card (transport plus museums) pays for itself.

  5. 5

    Getting around: walk, tram, ferry — and think before you bike

    The centre is small enough to walk. Trams and the metro cover the rest — tap in AND out with the same contactless card. The ferries behind Centraal to Noord are free. Uber works; official taxis (TCA) are metered and fine. Renting a bike is wonderful if you already cycle confidently in traffic — otherwise wait a day, watch how the locals ride, and stay out of the bike lanes on foot. Bikes have right of way, and they use it.

Ready-made days

  • Canal Ring & Old Centre

    The Dam to the Jordaan on foot — the classic first day

  • Museum Quarter Day

    Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh, Vondelpark, and lunch on the Albert Cuyp

  • Windmills & Waterland

    Zaanse Schans, Marken, Volendam and Edam in one loop

  • Haarlem & the Beach

    The prettier little sister, then the North Sea at Zandvoort

  • Across the Water to Noord

    Free ferries, the EYE, the Lookout and NDSM's street art

  • Harbour & Plantage

    NEMO, the maritime museum, the botanical garden and the zoo

  • Castles & Forts

    Muiderslot, the island fort of Pampus, and Naarden's star ramparts

  • Utrecht Day

    The Dom Tower, wharf cellars and a fairy-tale castle

  • Tulip Day (spring only)

    Keukenhof at opening, the bulb fields by bike, then Leiden

  • Rijksmuseum & Van Gogh on a Rainy Day

    The Night Watch, a free lunchtime concert, Van Gogh and the Stedelijk — a short dash apart

  • The Old Centre on a Rainy Day

    Antiquities, pancakes up a steep stair, a church in an attic, a canal-house home and a cat museum

  • The Jordaan on Foot

    Apple pie in a brown café, the Noordermarkt, Westerpark and Rembrandt's church — free to wander

  • Along the Amstel by Bike

    A windmill on the river, poffertjes in Ouderkerk and the forest park — for confident riders

  • Vondelpark, the Bos & the Goat Farm

    Playgrounds, the city's forest park, pancakes in a farmhouse and kid goats — almost free

  • A Taste of Amsterdam

    A famous cookie, herring and stroopwafels at the market, aged Gouda, a jenever tasting and a food hall

  • Amsterdam for Free

    A hidden courtyard, Dam Square, the free ferry, Eye's terrace, NEMO's roof and the library view

  • Alkmaar Cheese Market

    Friday mornings, April to September: the cheese carriers, the museum in the Waag and the old town

  • Dunes & North Sea by Bike

    Bikes from Haarlem into the dunes to see the bison, lunch on the beach and a swim at Zandvoort

  • The Jewish Cultural Quarter

    One ticket, a few flat minutes apart: the museum, the candle-lit synagogue, the Holocaust museum and the names monument

  • In Rembrandt's Footsteps

    His house and studio, the Anatomy Lesson's Waag, Saskia's church, the Night Watch and his last resting place

  • Canals by Boat, Easy Pace

    An open-boat cruise, lunch in the Nine Streets, the flower market and the Skinny Bridge — little walking
