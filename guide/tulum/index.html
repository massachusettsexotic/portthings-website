Arrival day: the essentials

The first day sets up the whole trip. Nine things worth doing, roughly in order.

  1. 1

    Get pesos from a bank ATM

    Skip the airport exchange booths. Use a real bank's ATM (BBVA, Santander, Banorte, HSBC) and always DECLINE the peso conversion offer — your bank's rate is better. Small bills matter: many places can't break a 500.

  2. 2

    Sort your phone data

    A prepaid Telcel chip costs ~150–250 pesos with data, no passport needed — any Telcel store or OXXO. Top up later at any OXXO (say 'recarga Telcel'). Check first whether your home plan already includes Mexico.

  3. 3

    Getting from the airport

    Cheapest solid option: the ADO bus — comfortable coaches to downtown Cancun, Playa, and Tulum from right outside arrivals. Book shuttles/taxis at official counters, not from people who approach you. Uber exists in Cancun but can't pick up at the airport.

  4. 4

    Do one big supermarket run

    Water, snacks, sunscreen, and breakfast basics cost a fraction of hotel-shop prices. Chedraui and Super Aki are the local go-tos; La Comer in Tulum is the upscale pick. Sunscreen tip: many cenotes and parks require biodegradable. And grab a little bottle of Microdyn near the produce — a few drops in water disinfect fresh fruit and vegetables before you eat them.

  5. 5

    Learn the taxi & colectivo game

    Taxis have no meters — agree on the price BEFORE getting in, in pesos. Colectivos (shared vans) run the coast constantly and cost a tenth of a taxi: flag one down, pay cash, say 'aquí por favor' to hop off.

    The Maya Museum, a planetarium show, air-conditioned malls and a Yucatecan dinner

  • Playa del Carmen on a Rainy Day

    The island museum, a chocolate workshop and a long lunch in town

  • Bacalar: the Lagoon of Seven Colours

  • Costa Maya: Chacchoben & Mahahual

  • Playa del Carmen for Next to Nothing

  • Cancún on a Bus Fare

    The public beach with the CANCÚN letters, market food downtown and the evening square

  • Río Lagartos Flamingos & the Pink Lakes

    A boat among the flamingos, the pink salt lakes, ceviche and a sunset on the estuary

    Breakfast burritos, the market, panuchos, seafood and sugar-cane mojitos at local prices
